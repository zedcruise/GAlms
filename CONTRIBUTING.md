# Contributing to GAlms

Thank you for helping develop GAlms. This project explores partnership between Human Advanced (HA) and Advanced Human (AH) intelligence, so contributions should be curious, constructive, and open to perspectives that challenge our assumptions.

## Before you contribute

Please search existing issues and pull requests before opening a new one. For substantial changes, open an issue first so the direction can be discussed before significant work begins.

By participating, you agree to keep discussion focused on ideas and implementation. Personal attacks, harassment, threats, discrimination, doxxing, and advocacy of violence are not welcome.

## Reporting an issue

When reporting a problem, include:

- A clear, specific title.
- The page or section where the issue occurs.
- Steps to reproduce the problem.
- What you expected to happen and what happened instead.
- Browser, operating system, screen size, and console errors when relevant.
- Screenshots or a short recording when they clarify the issue.

For philosophical or editorial concerns, explain the passage or interaction in question and suggest an alternative where possible.

## Pull requests

1. Fork the repository and create a focused branch from `main`.
2. Keep each pull request limited to one coherent change.
3. Preserve the project's direct, humorous, and philosophical voice without turning disagreement into personal criticism.
4. Update related copy or documentation when behavior or concepts change.
5. Check responsive layouts and accessibility considerations for UI changes.
6. Describe the change, motivation, testing performed, and any known limitations in the pull request.
7. Wait for review and respond constructively to requested changes.

A useful branch name might be `feature/perception-section`, `fix/mobile-navigation`, or `docs/contributing`.

## Content and design principles

Contributions should support the project's central themes:

- Treat HA and AH as complementary partners, not simplistic opposing teams.
- Favor inquiry, patience, and evidence over dogma or reaction.
- Make strong ideas understandable through plain language, useful metaphors, and appropriate humor.
- Avoid presenting speculation as settled scientific fact.
- Keep the global-democracy theme centered on equal human participation and nonviolence.
- Respect the historical and artistic context of referenced people, works, and events.

## Commit messages

Use concise imperative commit messages, for example:

- `Add perception section accessibility labels`
- `Fix mobile navigation spacing`
- `Clarify GAIMSS overview in README`

## License and attribution

By submitting a contribution, you confirm that you have the right to submit it and agree that it may be distributed under this repository's [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE). Do not commit copyrighted assets, personal data, credentials, or material that you do not have permission to distribute.

## Questions

If you are unsure whether an idea fits the project, open an issue with the `question` context before implementing it. Thoughtful disagreement is useful when it helps the project examine its assumptions without losing respect for the people involved.
